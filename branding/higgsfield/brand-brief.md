# TYBalls.ie visual identity brief

## Brand

TYBalls.ie is an Irish enquiry and planning service for Transition Year balls,
operated by DebsGuru Ltd. It helps school committees begin a practical
conversation about venue availability, dates, attendance, food, entertainment
and event requirements. It is not a ticket shop, venue directory or nightlife
brand.

The identity must feel established, calm and celebratory. It should be credible
to parents and schools while still feeling current to student committees.

## Core idea

**A well-planned night, from the first enquiry.**

The visual language should combine the considered detail of a printed event
invitation with the clarity of a modern service business. Celebration is shown
through composition, light, rhythm and material detail rather than glitter,
neon, confetti or nightclub clichés.

## Brand attributes

- Irish, contemporary and locally credible
- Warm without becoming childish
- Premium without suggesting luxury pricing
- Organised, direct and dependable
- Celebratory without alcohol or nightclub imagery
- Human without fabricated testimonials or documentary claims

## Typography direction

- Editorial serif for major headings and invitation-like moments
- Clear contemporary sans serif for navigation, forms and practical content
- No thin high-contrast lettering at small sizes
- No handwritten script for the primary wordmark
- The literal spelling must always be `TYBalls.ie`

Generated lettering is concept material only. The selected logo will be
redrawn as a clean vector so spelling, spacing and small-size rendering are
deterministic.

## Logo requirements

The selected system must support:

1. Primary horizontal wordmark: `TYBalls.ie`
2. Compact stacked lockup
3. `TY` monogram
4. Square favicon/app mark
5. One-colour navy version
6. One-colour white version
7. Navy, paper and transparent backgrounds
8. Legibility from a 16 px favicon to a large venue screen

Do not include a tagline in the permanent logo. The DebsGuru relationship is
displayed separately in site copy and legal information.

## Logo directions to generate

### Direction A — Editorial Rhythm

A confident typographic wordmark with a restrained custom serif treatment. A
single curved stroke may connect or rhythmically underline `TY` and `Balls`,
suggesting movement across a dance floor without becoming a literal dancer or
music note. The `.ie` should be clearly legible and visually integrated.

Best fit: primary recommendation. It balances committee appeal with adult
credibility and should translate well into a strong website header.

### Direction B — Invitation Seal

A compact monogram inspired by a contemporary printed invitation seal. Use the
letters `T` and `Y` as the actual construction, with a simple outer geometry and
generous negative space. Pair it with an understated sans-serif wordmark.

Avoid heraldry, crowns, shields, laurel wreaths, school crests and faux wax
textures. The result must feel Irish through restraint and material sensibility,
not through shamrocks or Celtic stereotypes.

### Direction C — Stage Mark

A geometric wordmark based on the spatial rhythm of a ballroom floor plan or
stage lighting grid. Use simple alignment, proportion and one distinctive
coral interruption. It should feel contemporary and operational rather than
technical or corporate.

Avoid spotlights, disco balls, equaliser bars and generic event-agency icons.

## Photography art direction

Photography should appear documentary and physically plausible, with natural
Irish venue architecture and restrained cinematic lighting. Prioritise empty or
pre-event spaces and close details:

- ballroom prepared shortly before doors open
- foyer or registration area
- table settings, printed place cards and menu details
- lighting and sound preparation without visible brand names
- committee planning materials viewed from above
- venue exterior or arrival area in Irish weather
- photobooth and photography preparation without identifiable guests

Initial launch imagery must not show identifiable minors. Do not imply that a
generated venue, supplier or attendee is a real TYBalls.ie client. Do not show
alcohol, alcohol branding, unsafe transport, medical staff or security claims.

Skin, hands, furniture, glassware, architecture, reflections, signage and
electrical equipment require close visual inspection before approval.

## Graphic and icon language

Use simple, weight-consistent forms derived from the approved monogram and its
corner radius. Decorative illustrations may be generated in Higgsfield, but
functional site icons will be rebuilt as accessible SVG or CSS components.

The first icon family covers:

- venue
- food and service
- music and production
- photography and extras
- date and availability
- attendance
- committee planning
- enquiry/contact

No text should be baked into informational illustrations. Important labels and
facts remain HTML content on the site.

## Motion direction

Motion must be quiet and deliberate:

- 3–5 second logo sting
- 6–8 second silent hero loop
- 10–15 second brand film
- 2–4 second decorative section loops

Use slow light changes, focus transitions, material movement or the construction
of the logo mark. Avoid rapid cutting, flashing, particle explosions, confetti
bursts and exaggerated 3D chrome. Every video needs a poster image and a static
alternative for reduced-motion users.

## Output and review rules

- Generate clean master images at the highest useful resolution, up to 4K.
- Generate logo concepts on flat backgrounds without mock-up scenes.
- Keep one concept per frame for accurate comparison and extraction.
- Never accept generated text without manually checking every character.
- Keep prompts, references, model, job URL, date and approval state recorded.
- Export site imagery as responsive AVIF/WebP with JPEG fallback when required.
- Export motion as WebM and MP4 with a compressed poster image.
- Do not publish an asset until it passes brand, factual, legal, accessibility
  and performance review.
